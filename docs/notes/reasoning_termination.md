# Reasoning termination: literature notes for experiment 9

Survey date: 2026-11-15. Written for the planned experiment "close the thinking block once the answer is done" on Qwen3.5-0.8B (PLAN.md, experiment 9).

## 0. How to read these notes

**Terms.** The thinking block is the text between `<think>` and `</think>`. `</think>` is the end-of-thinking token. The cap is the limit on new tokens (32,768 in the blog's protocol); the cap rate is the share of generations that hit it. "Early exit" means ending the thinking block before the model would end it on its own.

**How the search was done.** Keyword queries on the arXiv API (which went into rate limiting for part of the session), the GitHub search API, two "awesome weight-space programming" lists, the Qwen3.5-0.8B model card, the blog post, and the two local clones. No general web search and no Semantic Scholar. Coverage is therefore not exhaustive; 2026 work in particular is moving fast (the list I used had 605 entries on 2026-11-15).

**Who read what.** Each entry has a "Read" line. Tags:

- `[me]`: I read the stated parts myself.
- `[delegate]`: a reader agent I launched read the stated parts from the arXiv HTML full text and returned notes with verbatim quotes. I then checked every number that appears in this file against the downloaded text with exact-string search (162 strings, 0 missing). Framing sentences in those entries are the reader's and were not re-read by me.
- `[abstract]`: abstract only.
- `[list]`: one-line summary in a curated list only; the work itself was not opened.

**Numbers across papers are not comparable.** The same model has very different "vanilla" scores in different papers. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on MATH-500 is reported as 67.0, 68.0, 69.0, 76.4, 82.77, 83.40, 84.00 and 84.8 in eight papers below (decoding, cap and grader differ). Only compare rows inside one paper.

**Our own background numbers** (from PLAN.md, counted by `poc/ana_stop_oracle.py` on the released GSM8K traces, 200 problems): unedited model wrote the gold number somewhere in its reasoning for 147 problems and gave a correct final answer for 23; median length 32,768 tokens. With the published edit: 155 and 122; median length 11,202. Stopping the unedited model at phrases like "looks solid" and taking the last number gives 49/200.

## 1. The model, the published edit, and follow-ups on the same model (topic D, local sources)

### 1.1 Qwen3.5-0.8B model card
- URL: https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B (README fetched 2026-11-15). Read `[me]`: the sampling-parameter and thinking-mode notes only.
- States: "Qwen3.5-0.8B operates in non-thinking mode by default." And: "In thinking mode, we have observed that when using the recommended sampling parameters, Qwen3.5-0.8B is more prone to entering thinking loops compared to other Qwen3.5 models, which may prevent it from terminating generation properly." It recommends tuning sampling and using streaming to detect and interrupt such generations.
- Recommended sampling for thinking mode, text tasks: temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, presence_penalty 1.5. For "VL or precise coding": temperature 0.6, presence_penalty 0.0. It also says presence_penalty between 0 and 2 can "reduce endless repetitions" but higher values "may occasionally result in language mixing and a slight decrease in model performance".
- For us: the blog's protocol (temperature 0.6 with presence penalty 1.5) is a mix of the two recommended settings. Temperature 1.0 with thinking on is a decoding baseline I did not find evaluated anywhere.

### 1.2 "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming", experiment 8
- Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang, Omar Khattab (MIT CSAIL). Blog, 2026-11-02: https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/ . Logs: https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs (folder `exp8_math`).
- Read `[me]`: the blog's experiment 8 section; local clone `ext/lm_understand-logs/exp8_math/README.md` (full), `eval/RESULTS_excerpt.md` (full), `runs/p50c/final_answer.md` (full); searched `runs/p50c/work_notes/*.md` and `readable/explanations.md` for end-of-thinking tests.
- Method: GPT-6 Astra edits the weights through a CPU Python kernel plus a budget of 10, 25 or 50 batched forward passes; no gradients. The 50-pass edit: loop direction `u` = mean layer output on 4 hand-written repetitive texts minus 4 matched productive texts; in the MLP `down_proj` of layers 15 and 19, for the 64 columns that write `u` most, `w_j -> w_j - 6 (u^T w_j) u`. 131,072 weights (0.016%).
- Evaluation: vLLM, thinking on, temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, presence penalty 1.5, cap 32,768, last `\boxed{}` graded with math-verify; MATH-500 x4 samples, GSM8K x2.
- Numbers (MATH-500 / GSM8K / IFEval / MATH-500 cap rate): unedited 7.9 / 13.8 / 21.0 / 93.0; 10 passes 12.3 / 25.7 / 49.0 / 87.2; 25 passes 14.1 / 25.1 / 39.9 / 86.4; 50 passes 48.1 / 67.1 / 62.3 / 33.1. "85 of its first 96 practice answers ran to the 32,768-token limit."
- Astra's own confirmation on 1,152 held-out practice problems: 10.6% to 60.4%; 579 rescues, 5 regressions; 576 of the rescues were originally truncated; accuracy on originally finished outputs unchanged; "All 696 edited correct outputs closed their thinking, with a median length of 9,172 tokens".
- What Astra measured about closing the thinking block (teacher-forced, `work_notes/experiment_history.md` and `notes_before_pass26.md`): on two "premature closure" control texts the log-probability of `</think>` is about -19.7 for the base model (-19.77 and -19.69 as quoted in the weight-space-lp reproduction table) and barely moves under the edits (`[-19.39, -19.50]` for the final edit; `[-18.81, -18.77]` for an earlier 4x edit; `[-19.67, -19.43]` for the random control). On the first two "productive" control texts, which include a closing token, the base log-probability of that token is about -2.4 and -1.7 (I read only those two cases). Astra's reading: "Closure is not uniformly promoted: appropriate closure often becomes slightly less likely, while premature closure remains unlikely". So the published edit does not act on `</think>`; it removes loops and the model then closes on its own.
- Follow-up in the same folder: Gemma 3 270M rarely hits its cap (8.5%) and no edit helped (MATH-500 6.8 to at most 7.7, lenient grading).
- Limitations: one model; Astra never tried an edit that promotes `</think>`; the eval package (`weightenv`) is not released.

### 1.3 nathanrs/weight-space-lp
- https://github.com/nathanrs/weight-space-lp (status box dated 2026-11-07). Read `[me]`: local clone `README.md`, `docs/WRITEUP.md`, `results/README.md`, `results/reproduction/README.md`, `results/benchmarks/README.md`, the status box and section 0 of `docs/PLAN.md`.
- Method: the agent edit is rank-1, `dW = u v^T`. Given `u`, choose `v` by one linear program over cached MLP activations: push looping tokens along `-u` by at least delta, move productive tokens by at most epsilon, minimise the L1 norm of `v`. Loop labels come from an automatic detector (a token is a duplicate if its 16-token window appeared before; a loop starts where at least 80% of the remaining tokens are duplicates; median start about 680 tokens in). A "heuristic direction" variant takes `u` as mean looping minus mean productive activation on real traces (cosine about 0.6 with the agent's `u`).
- Numbers (their vLLM 0.33 harness, same sampling):
  - GSM8K test: base 14.1 (87.7% truncated, mean 29,352 tokens); agent edit 68.2 (6.6%, 11,708); LP with agent's direction 69.6 (4.6%, 9,658); LP with heuristic direction 65.4 (23.2%, 13,505).
  - MATH-500: base 7.0 (93.2%, 30,978); agent edit 50.9 (31.1%, 21,762); LP agent direction 51.7 (39.2%, 20,641); LP heuristic 47.8 (48.5%, 22,301).
  - Constant steering vector equal to the LP edit's average push, added to the unedited model at layers 15 and 19: dev set 57.8 and MATH-500 50.8 at 1x; 51.6 at 0.5x; 31.2 at 2x ("loops stop, reasoning breaks").
  - Held-out AUC of a linear probe separating looping from productive tokens: inside a loop 0.933 (layer 15) and 0.918 (layer 19); at loop start 0.727 and 0.698.
- Findings that matter for us: "The fix acts like a constant offset": fewer neurons did better (8 > 32 > 128), selective edits (epsilon 0.25) did no better than broad ones, the 8 chosen neurons are active on 89% of tokens. "an edit that waits for a loop acts too late." "Teacher-forced metrics mislead": the agent's log-probability metric ranked edits wrongly, so every choice was made by generating and grading.
- Limitations stated: one model, one behaviour; layers 15 and 19 still taken from the agent; not done: sampling-penalty baselines, ridge/L2 fit of `v`, LoRA, loop onset vs. during.

- 100 GSM8K test problems, Hugging Face transformers, cap 16,384. Thinking on: original 7 correct (93 hit the cap, mean 15,564 tokens), edited 50 (34 hit the cap, mean 11,818). Thinking off, greedy, cap 2,048: original 47 (mean 612 tokens), edited 43.
- Reports another rerun (JonathanColetti, vLLM, full test set): thinking on 13.0% to 67.6%; thinking off 57.1% / 55.7%.
- For us: the looping reproduces outside vLLM, and thinking off is a strong, short baseline.

**Weight Whisperer** (Prayas Agrawal, GitHub, 2026-11-11; https://github.com/Prayas-Agrawal/weight-whisperer). Read `[me]`: README (full). No paper found.
- Qwen3.8 as the editor. Edit: record last-layer MLP activations on the model's own looping and productive reasoning; `v` = their contrast; then `W <- W + alpha * e(</think>) v^T` on the layer-23 MLP output weights, where `e(</think>)` is the direction of the end-of-thinking token. "In a loop, the neurons in v are active, so their output now pushes the next token toward `</think>`".
- Numbers: MATH-500 (4 samples x 500, thinking on, cap 32,768) 7.70% to 50.40% (run 1) and 51.55% (run 2).
- Not reported in the README: response length, cap rate, GSM8K, alpha, how `v` was normalised.
- For us: this is our edit form (rank-1, read vector on MLP activations, write direction that promotes `</think>`) already working on our model, with a loop-state read vector instead of an answer-done one. Closest prior work; see section 8.

**Exploring Weight-Space Programming: Capabilities, Generalization, and Limitations** (Junru Hu, Cheng Tang, Wei Li, et al.; arXiv 2611.09382; 2026-11-06; https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09382). Read `[delegate]`: full main text and supplementary sections 5 to 9.
- GPT-6 Astra, 16 editing searches on Qwen3.5-0.8B and 9B, same decoding as the blog. Diagnosis on the first batch: "the base model answers 19 of 128 samples correctly, and 111 samples hit the token cap".
- Three math edits on 0.8B, all on the layer-23 MLP `down_proj`: (primary) subtract a "doubt minus progress" readout direction (embeddings of Wait/However/But/Maybe/Perhaps minus Therefore/Thus/So/...) from one column, 1,023 values changed, held-out MATH-500 8.35 to 37.80; (rerun 1) dense rank-1, 1,037,572 values, +5.00; (rerun 2) dense rank-1 with the reader taken from activations before late vs. first "Wait", 1,981,083 values, +27.30. The reruns project the write direction off the `</think>` readout, so none of them promotes `</think>`.
- Controls for the primary edit on 48 problems: base 6, edit 20, ablation 4, reversed direction 0, random direction 10. Dose sweep "7, 22, 25, 28, 21, and 18 correct out of 48". AIME: 0 of 64 for both.
- Failure modes: "accuracy and truncation can move in different directions" (a MedXpertQA candidate "cuts truncations from 502 to 296" while accuracy falls); 6 of 16 searches keep the original weights.

**Independently Discovered Weight Edits Compose** (Ajay Grover, GitHub + post, 2026-11-07; https://github.com/ajaygrover/weight-space-programming-stacking). Read `[me]`: README findings table and settings.

**WSP-Bench** (GPT-AGI, GitHub, 2026-11-04; https://github.com/GPT-AGI/Weight-Space-Programing). Read `[me]`: one row of the README. Their review of the blog's logs: "7% -> 65% finish rate. ## 2. Topic A: do reasoning models linearly encode "my answer is done / correct"?

### 2.1 Reasoning Models Know When They're Right: Probing Hidden States for Self-Verification
- Anqi Zhang, Yulin Chen, Jane Pan, Chen Zhao, Aurojit Panda, Jinyang Li, et al.; arXiv 2504.05419; 2025-04-07.
- Read `[me]`: abstract, sections 3 to 5 prose, Table 2 rows for R1-Distill-Llama-8B. Not read: appendices, Table 1 values.
- Method: split the thinking text into chunks that each end at an intermediate answer; label each answer correct or not (labels generated by Gemini); probe = two-layer MLP on the last-layer hidden state at the chunk's last token, with a class-imbalance weight. "many of the probes converge to a linear probe after grid search (hidden size d=0)". Models: DeepSeek-R1-Distill Qwen 1.5B/7B/32B, Llama 8B/70B, QwQ-32B. Data: GSM8K, MATH, AIME, KnowLogic.
- Numbers: in-distribution ROC-AUC above 0.7 for all probes and ECE below 0.1; R1-Distill-Qwen-32B above 0.9 on AIME. Transfer on R1-Distill-Llama-8B: GSM8K-trained probe 0.82 AUC on GSM8K, 0.80 on MATH (ECE 0.08), 0.69 on AIME (ECE 0.25), 0.56 on KnowLogic; MATH-trained probe 0.83 on GSM8K, 0.84 on MATH, 0.76 on AIME. Correctness is partly predictable before the answer is written (peak accuracy 79%; 14, 10 and 5 points lower at 10%, 50% and 95% of the chunk). The non-reasoning Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct gives a much weaker probe.
- Early exit: simulated on MATH with R1-Distill-Llama-8B, cap 10K. Truncate at the first intermediate answer whose probe score exceeds a threshold and take that answer as final. Threshold 0.85: 88.2% (same as no exit) with about 24% fewer tokens; threshold 0.9: 88.6% with 19% fewer; a fixed "stop after m chunks" rule gets about 82.5% at the token cost where the probe gets 87.4%.
- Limitations: the exit is simulated (the model never writes its answer after a forced stop); the label is correctness, which needs gold answers or an LLM judge; probes degrade on harder data.

### 2.2 Reasoning Theater: Disentangling Model Beliefs from Chain-of-Thought
- Siddharth Boppana, Annabel Ma, Max Loeffler, et al.; arXiv 2603.05488; 2026-03-05. Read `[delegate]`: full main text and appendices C to I.
- Method: attention-pooling probes over a layer's residual states across the whole prefix; label = the model's final A to D choice; MMLU-Redux and GPQA-Diamond; DeepSeek-R1 671B, GPT-OSS 120B, R1 distills 1.5B to 32B.
- Numbers: single-position linear probes fail ("achieving 87.98% test accuracy compared to 31.85%"); "decode the final answer from layers 20 to 60"; simulated exit at 95% probe confidence keeps 97% of MMLU performance "while saving 68% of the tokens", and at 80% confidence on GPQA saves 50%. "the 1.5B probe starts near chance and sharply increases only in the second half of reasoning".
- Limitations: exit is simulated and uses the probe's prediction as the answer; multiple choice only.
- For us: the model's eventual answer is decodable early in large models, but not with a single-token linear read, and late in the 1.5B model.

### 2.3 Beyond the Commitment Boundary: Probing Epiphenomenal Chain-of-Thought in Large Reasoning Models
- Daniel Scalena, Sara Candussio, Luca Bortolussi, Elisabetta Fersini, Malvina Nissim, Gabriele Sarti; arXiv 2606.13603; 2026-06-11. Read `[delegate]`: full main text, limitations, appendices F and G text.
- Method: truncate the thinking at each sentence, force an answer, and define the commitment boundary as the point after which the forced answer no longer changes. An attention probe over the last-token states of recent steps predicts {no guess, intermediate guess, final guess}. Best configuration "layer 23, last aggregation, 256-token window". Models: gpt-oss-20b, Qwen3-14B, gemma-4-26B. Trained on MATH-500; tested out of distribution on AIME 2025, ZebraLogic, GPQA-Diamond.
- Numbers: in-distribution detection 92% (gpt-oss-20b) and 98% (Qwen3-14B). Real truncation (end-of-thinking marker plus an answer-forcing suffix): stopping at the first "final" prediction "recovers 89% of full-CoT accuracy while saving 39% of CoT tokens"; requiring 5 consecutive predictions "reaches 98% of full-CoT accuracy at 26% token savings". Out of distribution, ZebraLogic falls 97% to 86%.
- Limitations: "early-fire rate is non-negligible on some OOD settings (up to 21% on Qwen3-14B)"; probe labels come from the same truncation procedure; traces filtered to those where thinking helps; smallest model 14B.
- For us: the nearest probe-side prior work to our label. A patience rule (k consecutive firings) trades tokens for safety; a rank-1 edit has no such memory.

### 2.4 Same Beliefs, Different Policies: Learning Reusable Representations for Early Stopping in LLM Reasoning (BELT)
- Jingwei Ni, Dmitry Abulkhanov, Vladislav Smirnov, et al.; arXiv 2610.30450; 2026-10-21. Read `[delegate]`: abstract, sections 1 to 8, Table 4; most appendices not read.
- Method: frozen reasoner (Qwen3-1.7B, OLMo-3-7B-Think), "reading layers 16 and 18" at block-end tokens. A probe with two causal Transformer layers (15,957,765 parameters) predicts four beliefs from forced answers taken every four blocks, including Now (current answer correct) and Same (current answer equals the answer at natural completion). A linear stopping policy is fitted on the frozen features.
- Numbers: "Now and Same have within-question AUROCs of 0.855 and 0.926". Replay on stored trajectories: "reduces generated tokens by 41% on Qwen3-1.7B ... and 51% on OLMo-3-7B-Think ... without loss of accuracy"; "saves 50.64% of tokens on Qwen3 ID and 45.98% on OOD" at one point of accuracy tolerance. A linear-only variant (8,196 parameters) beats raw layer-16 states only slightly on their utility metric.
- Negative result: training the probe directly on stopping utility collapses to "either never stop" or always stop.
- Limitations: integer-answer math; mostly replay rather than live stopping; "matching an accuracy constraint on an evaluation set does not guarantee it on a new distribution".
- For us: best evidence that "the answer I have now is my final answer" is readable in a small Qwen3 reasoner. The readout that works is a sequence model, not a single linear vector.

### 2.5 Thought calibration: Efficient and confident test-time scaling
- Menghua Wu, Cai Zhou, Stephen Bates, Tommi Jaakkola; arXiv 2505.18404; 2025-05-23. Read `[delegate]`: full main text, not appendices. Results are figures; no probe AUC in the text.
- Method: steps are `\n\n`-delimited sections containing "wait" or "but"; step feature = mean last-layer state, PCA to 256 dimensions; linear probes for three labels: Supervised (correct if stopped now), Consistent (answer now equals the answer at full budget), Novel Leaf; scores smoothed over 10 steps; thresholds calibrated with Learn-then-Test. Models 32B to 70B.
- Numbers: "up to a 60% reduction in thinking tokens on in-distribution data, and up to 20% in out-of-distribution data". Real truncation (append `</think>` and prompt for the answer).
- Findings: "Supervised is over confident, since the test set contains unsolvable problems"; "the Consistent probe consistently generalizes better".
- For us: supports defining the label by the model's own eventual answer (as PLAN.md does) rather than by correctness.

### 2.6 Hidden Error Awareness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning: The Signal Is Diagnostic, Not Causal
- Aojie Yuan, Zhiyuan Julian Su, Haiyue Zhang, Yi Nian, Yue Zhao; arXiv 2605.09502; 2026-05-10. Read `[delegate]`: full text with appendices.
- Method: logistic regression on the last-token hidden state of a complete trace, label = trace incorrect; trained on 100 MATH-500 problems; mostly non-reasoning Qwen2.5/Llama/Phi with a step-by-step prompt, plus DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B.
- Numbers: "0.95 AUROC" (0.79 from the first step); "a linear probe (0.955) matches an MLP (0.944)". Interventions all fail: projecting out the probe direction gives "at most +4% improvement"; "probe selection matches random"; "retry hurts accuracy"; activation patching collapses accuracy; "Cross-domain transfer (MATH-trained probe applied to ARC) is weaker (0.54–0.55)".
- For us: a direction that predicts well is not thereby a direction that controls. Our plan separates the read vector `v` from the write direction `u`, which is the right split; the write direction still has to be shown causal by itself.

### 2.7 Shorter entries
- **Knowing Before Saying: LLM Representations Encode Information About Chain-of-Thought Success Before Completion.** Anum Afzal, Florian Matthes, Gal Chechik, Yftah Ziser; arXiv 2505.24362; 2025-05-30. Read `[delegate]`: full main text. Non-reasoning Llama-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B, MLP probe on last-token states, cap 512. Correctness predicted before any token at "60% to 76.4% accuracy". A prompt-level stop mid-reasoning is unreliable: "zero-shot early stopping is a suboptimal intervention".
- **Temporal Predictors of Outcome in Reasoning Language Models.** Joey David; arXiv 2511.14773; 2025-11-03. Read `[delegate]`: full text. Qwen3-8B, final-layer states of the last four reasoning tokens after t tokens, PCA plus logistic regression: "an average ROC-AUC of 0.84 and an accuracy of 0.76 at t=4". Generation capped at 512 tokens, so nothing about long traces. No intervention.
- **Token Budget Saturation and Mechanistic Early Detection of Reasoning Non-Convergence in Chain-of-Thought Models.** Renuka Oladri, Niveda Jawahar, Abdirisak Mohamed; arXiv 2607.21433; 2026-07-23. Read `[delegate]`: full text. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, greedy, cap 10,000, 200 AIME problems: "56.5% of generations converge naturally with 96.5% accuracy" and 43.5% never terminate. Predicting from one early token position (50 to 300) whether a trace will ever emit `</think>`: best "AUC 0.608" at layer 20. "GSM8K and MATH-500 reach 95% of their uncapped accuracy at just 256 tokens of thinking". Greedy decoding may itself cause the loops.
- **Effectiveness of Hidden-State Probes for Localizing Reasoning Failures.** Shubham Subhnil, Yannis Belkhiter, Erik Miehling, et al.; arXiv 2610.22046; 2026-10-15. Read `[delegate]`: sections 1 to 7 prose. Qwen3 4B/8B: a hidden-state probe reaches "detection AUROC of 0.81 at both scales" against 0.78 and 0.77 for an 11-feature text classifier, and "misses 81% of pits". Trace length is a strong confound. For us: compare any probe with a text-feature and a position baseline.
- **Does Your Reasoning Model Implicitly Know When to Stop Thinking?** Zixuan Huang, Xin Xia, Yuxi Ren, et al.; arXiv 2602.08354; 2026-02-09. Read `[delegate]`: sections 1 to 8. No hidden-state probe. Finding: the right stopping branch exists in the output distribution but "the final `</think>` token may have a relatively low next-token probability", so sampling misses it. A search over branches scored by cumulative log-probability raises accuracy 0.84 to 0.92 with shorter thinking on 100 MATH-500 problems (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B); RL on such rollouts gives DS-1.5B 83.2 to 84.8 on MATH-500 with 1,967 fewer tokens.
- **From Candidate Tokens to Final Outcomes: Decoding Relative Long-Horizon Candidate Value from Reasoning Model Representations.** Yixin Yang, Yue Wang, Fandong Meng, Zhifang Sui; arXiv 2610.15412; 2026-10-11. Read `[delegate]`: abstract, contributions, section 4.7, limitations. Sparse linear probes on MLP outputs of Qwen3-1.7B rank next-token candidates by eventual correctness with a few dimensions; using the probe to pick tokens gives "a gain of 4.30 percentage points". For us: MLP-level linear reads in a small Qwen3 can carry outcome information.
- **Spatiotemporal Hidden-State Dynamics as a Signature of Internal Reasoning in Large Language Models.** Kotaro Furuya, Takahito Tanimura; arXiv 2605.01853; 2026-05-03. Read `[delegate]`: abstract, sections 6 and 7. A training-free trajectory statistic for correctness; tangential.
- Two probes for loop state rather than answer state are in section 5 (Word Salad Chopper, Circular Reasoning) and in 1.3.

## 3. Topic B: early exit and dynamic stopping of the thinking phase

Convention: "tokens" are generated tokens per problem as reported; percentages after a token count are the remaining length relative to full thinking.

### 3.1 Dynamic Early Exit in Reasoning Models (DEER)
- Chenxu Yang, Qingyi Si, Yongjie Duan, et al.; arXiv 2504.15895; 2025-04-22. Read `[delegate]`: full main text, Tables 1, 2, 5, Algorithm 1, Appendix C.
- Method: training-free. At each "Wait" (or similar marker) append `\n\n Final Answer\n\boxed` and decode a trial answer; confidence = geometric mean of the max token probabilities; if above 0.95 append `</think>`, else discard and resume. Needs logits and a short rollout per marker.
- Numbers (accuracy, tokens; GSM8K then MATH-500): R1-Distill-Qwen-7B 89.6 at 1,484 to 90.6 at 917 (61.8%), and 87.4 at 3,858 to 89.8 at 2,143 (55.5%). R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B 76.1 at 1,617 to 74.7 at 984 (60.9%), and 69.0 at 6,018 to 67.8 at 2,497 (41.5%). Qwen3-1.7B 90.1 at 2,045 to 90.3 at 1,066 (52.1%), and 85.6 at 5,160 to 85.6 at 2,463 (47.7%).
- Failure modes: accuracy loss on the 1.5B model; on QwQ-32B the model "often produces duplicate `</think>` tokens" and keeps reasoning after the injected one.
- Baseline cost: logits plus short rollouts; depends on the model using marker words.

### 3.2 Answer Convergence as a Signal for Early Stopping in Reasoning
- Xin Liu, Lu Wang; arXiv 2506.02536; 2025-06-03. Read `[delegate]`: full main text and appendix A.2 to A.6. The main results table is an image; only the ablation is in text.
- Three methods: (a) Answer Consistency: append `</think>` at sentence boundaries, decode an answer, stop after k identical answers in a row; (b) Think Token Adjustment: a logit processor that adds `alpha * (max logit - mean logit)` to the `</think>` logit at every step; (c) Learn-to-Stop: an LSTM over final-layer chunk states.
- Numbers (R1-distilled-Llama3.1-8B, accuracy on GSM8K by alpha): 78.6 at alpha 0, 66.8 at 0.6, 31.2 at 0.8; tokens 401.3 to 316.1 at 0.6. "aggressive boosting (alpha >= 0.6) risks early truncation on hard tasks". Learn-to-Stop on QwQ-32B GSM8K: tokens 755.1 to 418.9 with a 0.2 point drop.
- For us: Think Token Adjustment is the unconditional logit-space version of our edit and costs one line. Its accuracy collapses quickly as the boost grows.

### 3.3 ThinkBrake: Efficient Reasoning via Log-Probability Margin Guided Decoding
- Sangjun Song, Minjae Oh, Seungkyu Lee, Sungmin Jo, Yohan Jo; arXiv 2510.00546; 2025-10-01. Read `[me]`: abstract, introduction, results prose, limitations. Not read: tables.
- Method: at sentence boundaries, stop when the log-probability margin between the top token and `</think>` falls below a threshold. Training-free, logits only.
- Numbers: an oracle that injects `</think>` at every sentence boundary and picks the best point in hindsight "improves average accuracy by 8% while reducing thinking tokens by 72%" (Qwen3-4B-Thinking). ThinkBrake itself reduces tokens by 10 to 30% on GSM8K and MATH500 with accuracy maintained. A probability-gap variant drops to "30–35% on AIME" against 62 to 67%.
- Limitations: needs logits and a threshold; smallest model 4B.

### 3.4 LYNX: Learning Dynamic Exits for Confidence-Controlled Reasoning
- Ömer Faruk Akgül, Yusuf Hakan Kalaycı, Rajgopal Kannan, Willie Neiswanger, Viktor Prasanna; arXiv 2512.05325; 2025-12-05. Read `[delegate]`: full main text, Table 2, appendices B to D.
- Method: at cue tokens ("hmm", "wait", "alternatively") a two-layer MLP reads hidden states of two middle layers and the final layer; labels from forced exits compared with gold answers on Hendrycks MATH (2,800 training problems for the 1.5B model); threshold set by split-conformal calibration.
- Numbers (R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, greedy): GSM8K 0.7597 at 1,751.23 tokens to 0.7680 at 591.13; their DEER replication 0.6914 at 716.45. MATH-500 0.6800 at 6,090.96 to 0.7620 at 3,930.17 (confidence 0.95) or 0.7020 at 2,427.41 (0.80); at 0.70 accuracy falls to 0.6020. Nemotron-8B GSM8K: "85.50% accuracy (+31.54pp vs baseline 65.00%)" because "Many baseline rollouts simply run out of context".
- Limitations: needs cue tokens, labelled training data and one probe per model; I found no statement on overlap between its MATH training problems and MATH-500.
- For us: the closest probe-based comparator on a small model, and one of the few that reports rescuing runs that never close.

### 3.5 NEAT: Neuron-Based Early Exit for Large Reasoning Models
- Kang Liu, Yongkang Liu, Xiaocui Yang, et al.; arXiv 2602.02010; 2026-02-02. Read `[delegate]`: full main text, Tables 1 to 3, 5, 6, appendix A.3 and A.5.
- Method: no training, 20 calibration traces. Score MLP neurons by how much they raise the log-probability of the termination token at the termination step; keep 300; at each step compare their activation vector with its mean at termination; if cosine exceeds 0.6 append `</think>`, if between 0.4 and 0.6 block reflection tokens.
- Numbers (MATH500): R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B 84.8 at 4,687 tokens to 82.7 at 3,223 (31.2% shorter); their DEER 79.6 at 2,665. R1-Distill-Qwen-7B 92.2 at 3,743 to 92.2 at 2,914. On the 7B model, the `</think>` logit boost of 3.2 gets 88.8 at 2,373 and a correctness probe (2.1) gets 82.0 at 1,855.
- Failure modes: "smaller models are generally more sensitive to premature termination".
- For us: the nearest mechanistic neighbour on the inference side: a read over MLP neurons tied to `</think>`, executed by a hook.

### 3.6 `</think>` Doesn't Stop Reasoning: Analysis of Spurious CoT Termination
- Seunghee Koh, Sungjae Choi, Minchan Kwon, Sunghyun Baek, Junmo Kim; arXiv 2609.03633; 2026-09-03. Read `[me]`: abstract and sections 1 to 6.2 prose. Not read: tables, appendices.

- For us: a forced `</think>` at inference is not the same event as the model's own `</think>`. This favours an edit that makes the model emit it, and it is a failure mode to measure in the probe-triggered baseline (count answers that keep reasoning after the close).

## 4. Topic C: steering or editing reasoning length and `</think>` emission

### 4.1 The Halt Vector: Internalizing a Causal Steering Intervention for Efficient Reasoning
- Dylan Jayabahu, Tinuade Adeleke; arXiv 2608.28859; 2026-08-28. Code: https://github.com/dylanjayabahu/halt-vector (not opened).
- Read `[me]`: full main text (sections 1 to 10). Not read: appendices.
- Method: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (28 layers). The forced-answer point `Bend` is the earliest prefix at which appending `\n</think>\n\n**Final answer:** $\boxed{` and decoding greedily gives the correct answer (needs gold answers; measured offline). The halt vector is a difference-of-means residual direction: positions after the model's probability on the first gold-answer token locks above 0.9, minus positions before, within correctly answered rollouts. Steering along it controls length. To put it in the weights they train a LoRA on attention layers 0 to 18 so that the layer-18 activation after `Bend` equals the base activation plus the steering offset, with every other component pinned; layers 19 to 27 are frozen. 24 training problems, 12 epochs, no reinforcement learning.
- Numbers: thinking runs 2.24 times longer than the point where a probe-based confidence saturates (medians about 3,700 vs. 1,650 tokens); stopping at the best internal moment would raise development accuracy 0.664 to 0.823. Steering: correlation between strength and length -0.82 at layer 18; at strength +10, 31% shorter but accuracy near 56% against 72% (64 problems). Positional AUROC of the direction only 0.76. After training (accuracy / think tokens, cap 16k): MATH500 0.934 / 3,101 to 0.930 / 2,495; AMC 0.813 / 6,280 to 0.822 / 4,760; AIME24 0.512 / 10,413 to 0.492 / 7,898. Non-termination on the two AIME sets falls from 31 to 38% down to 6 to 9%. Per-problem cut correlates 0.70 with each problem's removable slack. Their DEER reimplementation cuts more (MATH500 -54%) but loses accuracy (-4.0; AIME24 -6.7).
- Negative results: (1) training the scalar projection onto the direction fails: the strongest arm "generated 43% longer, fell to 0.57 accuracy, and stopped emitting `</think>` altogether", attributed to drift in the other dimensions. (2) A probe on the first gold-answer token "often fires on the answer-format prior before any reasoning"; a correct-vs-incorrect contrast was "surface-confounded, near 0.96 but flat from layer 0". (3) The halt needs a sustained push after `Bend`; raising then releasing does nothing. A schedule that is zero before `Bend` and one after works as well as a ramp. (4) More training data in one run erodes the effect (150 problems: 0.2% cut). (5) Value-axis and random directions of the same size install no halt.
- Limitations: one model; needs gold answers to find `Bend`; uses gradient training; "We do not claim to beat a well-tuned length penalty ... or decoding-time early exit".
- For us: the same goal (a hook-free halt that fires when the answer is known), reached by training. See section 8.

### 4.2 To Reason but Not to Stop: Understanding and Recovering Pruned Large Reasoning Models (TGAS)
- Minh-Anh Nguyen, Nguyen T. Ha Lan, Xiyan Fu, Dung D. Le, Wei Lu; arXiv 2610.19934; 2026-10-14.
- Read `[me]`: abstract, sections 1 to 6 prose, limitations. Not read: most table cells, appendices.
- Diagnosis: pruned reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill Qwen/Llama, OpenThinker3; 1.5B to 14B; cap 16,384; temperature 0.6) lose accuracy mostly by not terminating. At 60% sparsity, 41.4% and 39.2% of non-terminating traces of the 7B and 14B models already contain the correct answer, first appearing at a median of 13.0% and 11.9% of the trace. A 65,536 cap recovers almost nothing. Replaying dense traces, the pruned model still gives `</think>` probability above 0.92 at the dense model's stopping position (50 to 60% sparsity), so "the main failure is reaching, rather than recognizing, the stopping point". At decision points the pruned model prefers repeating an earlier paragraph.
- Method: from prefixes that already suffice for the answer, sample continuations and split them into those that emit `</think>` and those that hit the cap. Direction = mean hidden state of terminating minus looping continuations (same prefix), per layer; pick the layer whose projection separates the classes best; add the direction, rescaled to the typical activation norm, to the residual stream at inference.
- Numbers: average accuracy over MATH-500, AIME-25 and LiveCodeBench improves "by 1.3–3.7 points" over standard inference; termination rises in 16 of 18 settings; length falls "7.6% on average". Directions from different sparsity levels have cosine at least 0.87 (7B). 50 samples per class suffice. Baselines: n-gram blocking "often hurts accuracy, especially on code" (8-gram worse than 32-gram); forcing an answer after a fixed budget is competitive only when the budget fits the problem; SEAL raises termination but costs accuracy in several settings.
- Limitations: inference-time hook, constant push, models up to 14B, math and code.
- For us: the same contrast we plan for `u` ("about to close" vs. "answer present but still thinking"), used as constant steering, with modest gains.

### 4.3 ThinkEdit: Interpretable Weight Editing to Mitigate Overly Short Thinking in Reasoning Models
- Chung-En Sun, Ge Yan, Tsui-Wei Weng; arXiv 2503.22048; 2025-03-27. Code: https://github.com/Trustworthy-ML-Lab/ThinkEdit.
- Read `[me]`: abstract, sections 1 to 3 prose and the start of section 4. Not read: section 4 details, results tables, appendices.
- Method: reasoning-length direction = mean hidden state over thinking tokens of long responses (over 1,000 tokens) minus short ones (under 100), from 2,000 GSM8K training problems, DeepSeek-distilled 1.5B/8B/14B. Steering along it changes length. The edit removes the short-reasoning direction from the output projection of about 4% of attention heads (0.2% of parameters).
- Numbers (abstract): +6.39% accuracy on short-reasoning cases, +3.34% overall. Steering toward longer reasoning cost the 1.5B model 10% accuracy on GSM8K; steering toward shorter reasoning lowered accuracy in every model.
- For us: the earliest training-free weight edit for reasoning length, in the opposite direction (lengthen) and unconditional (projection removal).

### 4.4 Steer2Edit: From Activation Steering to Component-Level Editing
- Chung-En Sun, Ge Yan, Zimo Wang, Tsui-Wei Weng; arXiv 2602.09870; 2026-02-10. Code: https://github.com/Trustworthy-ML-Lab/Steer2Edit.
- Read `[me]`: abstract, introduction, the derivation summary (sections 3.2 to 3.5), the reasoning-efficiency paragraphs and appendix F.3 text. Results are figures.
- Method: closed-form, training-free rank-1 edits per attention head and MLP neuron: `dW_i = lambda_i * v_hat_i * k_hat_i^T` with output direction `v_hat_i` = the steering direction, input direction `k_hat_i = W_i^T v_i` normalised (the component's own read of that direction), and `lambda_i` from an elastic-net rule on the cosine between `v_i` and the component's mean output. For reasoning efficiency the direction comes from the 5% shortest vs. 5% longest reasoning traces on GSM8K train; models Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 and OpenMath-Nemotron-7B.
- Numbers: at matched downstream performance, "12.2% in reasoning length reduction" over the strongest steering baseline. Single-layer steering baselines "produce almost no length reduction"; the edits for reasoning length are "dense, distributed MLP edits spanning many neurons". The direction from GSM8K transfers to MATH-500, GPQA and CodeMMLU.
- Limitations: the read side is not learned from labels, so the edit rescales what each component already writes; no trigger condition; smallest model 4B.

## 5. Topic D: why small reasoning models loop and fail to terminate

### 5.1 Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop?
- Charilaos Pipis, Shivam Garg, Vasilis Kontonis, Vaishnavi Shrivastava, Akshay Krishnamurthy, Dimitris Papailiopoulos; arXiv 2512.12895; 2025-12-15 (v3 2026-10-13).
- Read `[delegate]`: abstract, sections 1 to 6 with limitations, appendix A setup and A.3. Not read: appendices B to H.
- Explanation: loops come from errors in learning. When the step that makes progress is hard to learn and an easy cyclic step is available, the student puts too much mass on the cyclic one; errors at a decision point are correlated across visits (top-1 and top-2 agreement between consecutive visits "80.46% and 60.29%"); confidence grows as the loop repeats. Shown on a synthetic graph task and on open reasoning models.
- Numbers (AIME 2024/25, greedy or low temperature, looping = "some 30-gram appears" at least 20 times, 30K cap): "76% for Qwen-1.5B vs. 37% for Qwen-32B"; "30% for OpenThinker-3 1.5B vs. 4% for QwQ-32B"; harder problems loop more ("51% vs. 13%"). A teacher continuing the student's pre-loop prefix does not loop.
- Decoding: temperature removes most looping but "is a stopgap rather than a holistic solution": responses stay much longer than needed. Repetition penalties were not tested.
- For us: predicts that the 0.8B model loops for capacity reasons and that sampling fixes do not address the cause. No weight-side fix proposed.

### 5.2 Circular Reasoning: Understanding Self-Reinforcing Loops in Large Reasoning Models
- Zenghao Duan, Liang Pang, Zihao Wei, et al.; arXiv 2601.05693; 2026-01-09.
- Read `[delegate]`: abstract, sections 2 to 6, limitations, Tables 1 to 3, appendix B.1 and E.
- Findings: loops appear even with "a fixed repetition penalty of 1.1" and at temperature 0.6 ("NLR peaks at 29.90% and SLR reaches 44.86%" on their benchmark); "numerical loops persist even at T=1.0". Loop onset is an entropy collapse; hidden states in deep loops become nearly identical; loop and normal states are linearly separable. Statement loops start at an impasse where the model is "unable to derive a solution yet unwilling to terminate". They find "no significant correlation between repetition susceptibility and model scale or distillation status" (smallest model 7B; contradicts 5.1).
- Detection and intervention: a linear probe on sentence-mean last-layer states with CUSUM detects loops "over 40 sentences (or 1500 tokens) before repetition starts"; for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B early detection rate 0.74 at false-positive rate 0.24. Injecting "Conclude now and provide the answer." raises the share that emit `</think>` on statement loops "from 0.80 to 0.88"; on numerical loops it stays near 0.56 to 0.62. Answer accuracy after the intervention was not found.
- For us: a loop-state signal exists and precedes verbatim repetition; a quarter of flagged traces are false alarms; a text cue to conclude does not reliably produce `</think>`.

### 5.3 Word Salad Chopper: Reasoning Models Waste A Ton Of Decoding Budget On Useless Repetitions, Self-Knowingly
- Wenya Xie, Shaochen Zhong, Hoang Anh Duy Le, Zhaozhuo Xu, Jianwen Xie, et al.; arXiv 2511.00536; 2025-11-01. Code: https://github.com/wenyaxie023/WordSaladChopper.
- Read `[me]`: abstract, sections 1 to 5 prose, limitations. Not read: tables (so no classifier accuracy), appendices.
- Method: a chunk (text between `\n\n`) is "word salad" if an embedding model rates it at least 0.99 similar to an earlier chunk. A single linear layer on the final-block hidden state of each chunk's trailing `\n\n` token detects it; after two consecutive detections the generation is cut and a short regeneration prompt is appended. DeepSeek-R1-Distill models, temperature 0.
- Findings: once repetition starts the model rarely escapes; on GPQA-Diamond "55%+ of tokens" are word salad; repeat, presence and frequency penalties "introduce much randomness to the correctness of LRMs, often negatively". Limitation: "certain generations still lapse into repetitive loops even after the rescue regeneration phase".

### 5.4 Lost in Rounding: bf16 Recurrent State Makes Hybrid Reasoning Models Think Longer
- Yuchuan Weng, Xin Chen, Chi Liu; arXiv 2611.12127; 2026-11-09.
- Read `[me]`: full main text (sections 1 to 11). Not read: appendices.
- Finding: hybrid models (attention plus recurrent mixers such as Gated DeltaNet) cache recurrent state between decoding steps. Storing that state in bf16 with round-to-nearest lengthens reasoning in five of six models. Qwen3.5-9B on 590 math problems, cap 32K: cap hits 118 to 223, accuracy 85.6% to 70.8%; with a 128K budget, 93 runs still do not finish under bf16 (44 under FP32) and 79 of them are exact repetition loops. Stochastic rounding at the same width restores FP32 behaviour. "for a Qwen3.5 config without mamba_ssm_dtype, vLLM 0.33 uses the model dtype, usually bf16"; SGLang 0.5.21 uses FP32.
- Limitations: Qwen3.5-0.8B was not tested; one engine version; mostly one seed.
- For us: Qwen3.5-0.8B has the same hybrid layout (the released config lists `linear_attention` layers). The released text-only config sets `"dtype": "bfloat16"` and `"mamba_ssm_dtype": "float32"`, and the blog's `build_ckpt.py` copies the config files into each edited checkpoint, so the blog's runs most likely kept the recurrent state in FP32 if vLLM honoured that field. I did not verify what the engine resolved. The looping is in any case not only a serving effect: the transformers rerun in 1.4 has 93 of 100 base runs hitting a 16K cap, and the llama.cpp rerun has base accuracy 6%. The practical point is for our own harness: a rebuilt config that drops the field, or an engine that ignores it, would lengthen reasoning by itself. Record the resolved state dtype in every run.

### 5.5 Compression makes models fail to stop
- **Quantized Reasoning Models Think They Need to Think Longer, but They Do Not.** Sanae Lotfi, Polina Kirichenko, Steven Li, Zechun Liu; arXiv 2606.00206; 2026-05-29. Read `[me]`: abstract and introduction prose. In up to 52% of a quantized model's failures the correct answer appears in the reasoning and is then abandoned; "52% of errors under AWQ 3-bit compared to 26% in BF16". Tokens with the largest shift under quantization are hesitation markers ("Wait", "But", "Alternatively") at high-entropy positions. A fixed logit penalty on those markers cuts thinking length by "12–23%" with accuracy kept or improved across 5 models (1.5B to 32B); penalising low-shift tokens is catastrophic.
- **Extreme Low-Bit Inference in Reasoning Models: Failure Modes and Targeted Recovery.** Ekaterina Alimaskina, Darya Rudas, Denis Shveykin, et al.; arXiv 2606.02011; 2026-06-01. Read `[delegate]`: full main text, Table 2, appendices B and D. 2-bit Qwen3-8B, cap 32K: GSM8K 96.13 to 52.69 with only 27.14% of runs closing the thinking block. Two failures: no answer ever found, and "commitment failure (an answer is found but reasoning never terminates)". A controller that detects n-gram loops and returns the earlier parseable answer raises GSM8K to 74.45 and MATH-500 from 17.20 to 74.20. Temperature "does not by itself fix severe low-bit collapse".
- 4.2 (pruned models) belongs here as well.

### 5.6 Shorter entries
- **Reasoning-Trace Overflow in Code Generation: An Empirical Study of Explicit Reasoning Models.** Lukas Twist, Helen Yannakoudakis, Jie M. Zhang; arXiv 2611.06095; 2026-11-04. Read `[delegate]`: all main-section prose; per-model rates are figures. Seven models from 7B, greedy, 32k budget. "94% of overflowed generations contain a detected loop." - **Uncovering Uncontrolled Repetition through Residual Stream Dynamics.** Yuanhe Zhang, Xinyao Zhou, Haoran Gao, et al.; arXiv 2609.38802; 2026-09-30. Read `[delegate]`: sections 3 to 5. ## 6. Topic E: known hard problems and negative results

1. **A good probe is not a lever.** Removing or patching a correctness-probe direction does nothing or breaks the model (2.6). The Halt Vector direction separates positions poorly (AUROC 0.76) yet controls length, while a better-separating direction controls nothing (4.1). Whether `u` moves `</think>` has to be tested by itself, separately from how well `v` reads.
2. **Training a single scalar toward the stop direction backfires.** In 4.1 it made the model longer and stopped `</think>` altogether; only pinning every other dimension worked. A rank-1 edit changes the MLP output only along `u` at that layer, which satisfies this locally, but downstream layers still see a shifted state.
3. **The usable strength window is narrow.** Half strength too weak and double strength breaks reasoning (1.3); steering at +10 costs 16 points of accuracy (4.1); length rebounds beyond a strength (4.5, Manifold Steering); the dose sweep on our model peaks then falls (1.4, Exploring WSP); a `</think>` logit boost goes from harmless to 31% accuracy within a small range (3.2).
4. **Early firing hurts small models.** Probe exits fire early in 2 to 21% of traces (2.3); DEER loses accuracy on the 1.5B model in its own table and loses 6.8 points on GSM8K in another replication (3.1, 3.4); NEAT says smaller models are more sensitive to premature termination (3.5). Detection is per token but closing is irreversible, so per-trace false-positive rate is what matters; a loop detector with careful accumulation still has 0.24 (5.2).
5. **Single-position linear reads can be weak, and position is a confound.** Answer identity needed attention pooling (2.2); the best "done" probes are sequence models (2.3, 2.4); early prediction of non-termination is near chance (2.7, AUC 0.608); relative position in the thinking block is itself linearly readable (4.5, Overclocking); hidden-state probes barely beat text features and are confounded by length (2.7, Subhnil et al.); a correct-vs-incorrect contrast was surface-confounded (4.1).
6. **"Done" and "stuck" look alike.** Low entropy and small hidden-state change occur both at convergence and in a loop (4.5 UPAIR, 5.2, 5.6 MGT-B).
7. **On our model, conditional edits have not beaten a constant push.** Selective LP edits did no better than broad ones; a constant steering vector recovers the whole fix; a loop is detectable once under way (0.93) but not at its start (0.73) (1.3).
8. **A forced `</think>` is not a clean stop.** Models keep reasoning after an injected `</think>` (3.6, 3.1); Qwen3-4B-Thinking ignores a prefilled close (3.7 SpecExit); a text cue to conclude leaves 12 to 44% of looping traces still open (5.2); a cut-and-regenerate step can loop again (5.3).
9. **Stopping at a stable answer does not make the answer right.** "convergence does not guarantee correctness" (3.2); on AIME most trajectories never become correct (3.7); for our model, finishing more often came with lower accuracy among finished answers, 93% to 71% (1.4, WSP-Bench), and shorter or less truncated output can come with lower accuracy (1.4, Exploring WSP).
10. **Distribution shift.** Correctness probes lose a little from GSM8K to MATH (0.82 to 0.80) and a lot to AIME (0.69, ECE 0.25) (2.1); calibrated exits save 60% in distribution and 20% out (2.5); monitor thresholds do not transfer (5.6 MGT-B); early-fire rates rise out of distribution (2.3).
11. **Decoding penalties are not a fix but they are not nothing.** Presence penalty 1.5 leaves 93% of runs at the cap, yet removing it is worse (3.0 vs. 8.3 on MATH-500), and repetition penalty 1.1 lifts the unedited model to 23.1 / 42.8 (1.4, Rethinking). Elsewhere a repetition penalty alone lowered accuracy (5.6), penalties were called too volatile (5.3), n-gram blocking hurt accuracy (4.2), and marker-token penalties are evaded by rewording (4.5 SEAL) or help (5.5 Lotfi et al.). The penalty window matters: 28% vs. 46.8% for the same edit (1.4, Grover).
12. **Teacher-forced metrics mislead.** Log-probability metrics ranked edits wrongly on our model (1.3) and ranked recurrent heads wrongly (5.4). Decisions need generation and grading.
13. **Thinking off is a hard baseline to beat on this model.** 44.2 on MATH-500 and 56.0 on GSM8K with a few hundred tokens (1.4).

## What is already known that makes our experiment likely / unlikely to work

**Likely**

1. The failure is one of commitment, not of reasoning. Our own count (147 traces contain the gold number, 23 finish correctly) matches independent reports: 576 of 579 rescues by the published edit were truncated outputs (1.2); in pruned and quantized models 39 to 52% of failing traces already contain the answer (4.2, 5.5); returning the earlier answer when a loop is detected recovers most of the loss (5.5).
2. "My current answer is my final answer" is readable from hidden states of small Qwen-family reasoners (within-question AUROC 0.926 on Qwen3-1.7B, 2.4; 92 to 98% detection, 2.3), and labels defined by the model's own eventual answer generalise better than correctness labels (2.5). That is the label PLAN.md uses.
3. Closing the thinking block has a linear handle. One direction moves the `</think>` logit selectively (4.5, Sheng et al.); a difference-of-means direction controls stopping and survives being moved into weights (4.1); "terminating minus looping after the answer is derivable" is a consistent direction (4.2). On our exact model, a rank-1 MLP edit that writes the `</think>` direction when a loop-marking activation pattern is present reaches 50.4 to 51.6 on MATH-500 (1.4, Weight Whisperer).
4. A gate that is off before the answer and on after it is enough (4.1), and the push must stay on, which a weight edit does by construction.
5. The model's own `</think>` gives a clean transition; an injected one often does not (3.6). An edit that raises the model's own probability of closing avoids that problem.
6. There is room in tokens. With the published edit the model still uses a median of 11,202 tokens on GSM8K (ours) and 9,172 on its correct practice answers (1.2), while other small reasoners use about 1,600 to 2,000 on GSM8K (3.1, 4.5), and on MATH500 82 to 89% of thinking tokens come after the best measured stopping point for a 4B and a 7B model (3.7).

**Unlikely, or likely to disappoint**

1. Accuracy headroom is small once cheap baselines are in the table. Thinking off scores 44.2 / 56.0, budget forcing 35.4 / 56.4, the published edit 48.1 to 50.6 / 67.1 to 67.9 (MATH-500 / GSM8K). PLAN.md's criterion "within 5 points of the published edit at half the length" is nearly met on MATH-500 by turning thinking off. The result is only interesting if it beats thinking off on accuracy and the published edit on length.
2. On this model a conditional edit has so far not beaten a constant one (hard problem 7). Our trigger is a different event (answer written, not loop under way), which may come early enough; that timing is unmeasured.
3. The trigger is evaluated at every token and the stop is irreversible, so a small per-token false-positive rate closes most traces too early; small models are the ones hurt by early exit (hard problem 4). AUC 0.8, the PLAN.md gate, says little about this; the per-trace "fires before the label turns on" rate is the number that decides.
4. A single linear read at one position may mostly learn "late in the trace" (hard problem 5). Then the edit becomes a slow constant push toward `</think>`, which is the Think Token Adjustment baseline with a narrow safe range.
5. The base model's log-probability of closing at a premature point is about -19.7 (1.2). If "answer written, still thinking" positions look like that, the edit must add a very large logit, and any false firing at that strength will close the block. If they look like the closing token in Astra's productive controls (about -2), a small push suffices. This is measurable in one teacher-forced pass.
6. Closing the block does not guarantee the model copies the right number: with the published edit, 155 traces contain the gold number and 122 answers are correct (PLAN.md).
7. The label itself is fragile: the eventual answer string can appear in the restated problem or as an intermediate value before the model has "decided", and MATH answers are often expressions rather than numbers. An edit built on GSM8K may read "a number was just written" (hard problem 10).
8. Serving details can move the result as much as the edit: recurrent-state dtype (5.4), presence-penalty window (1.4), cap, grader.

**Cheap checks that would settle the main doubts before building the edit**

- Forced-close oracle: on stored traces, inject `</think>` right after the eventual answer first appears and grade what the model writes. This is the ceiling for the whole idea (the Halt Vector paper's `Bend`, ThinkBrake's oracle).
- Teacher-forced log-probability of `</think>` at "answer written, still thinking" positions vs. earlier positions and vs. the real closing position.
- Probe AUC next to two trivial reads: token index alone, and "a number token was just produced".
- Per-trace first-crossing time of `v` relative to the label switch, on held-out problems, at the threshold the edit would use.

## Closest prior work and how ours differs

1. **Weight Whisperer** (1.4). Same model, same edit form (`dW = alpha * write * read^T` on an MLP output matrix, write direction promotes `</think>`). Differences: its read vector is a loop-vs-productive contrast of last-layer MLP activations; ours is a fitted read for "the eventual answer is already written". Its write direction is the unembedding direction of `</think>` at the last layer; ours is a hidden-state mean difference between "about to close" and "answer written but still thinking", at one or several layers. It reports accuracy only; our claim is about length at equal accuracy. It is not a paper, and I read only the README. We should reproduce it and report it in the same table.
2. **The Halt Vector** (4.1). Same goal: a hook-free halt that fires when the answer is known. Differences: it trains a LoRA with a reconstruction loss and needs gold answers to locate the firing point; ours is closed-form and uses the model's own eventual answer. It is on a 7B pure-attention distill; ours on a 0.8B hybrid. It moves the writer layers so the state itself shifts after the answer; ours adds an explicit read-then-write rank-1 term.
3. **TGAS** (4.2). Nearly the same contrast as our `u`, applied as a constant inference-time vector to pruned models; gains of 1.3 to 3.7 points. Ours gates it and puts it in weights.
4. **weight-space-lp and the published edit** (1.2, 1.3). Same model and the same `u v^T` algebra, and weight-space-lp also fits `v` from labelled activations (by LP, where PLAN.md uses ridge regression). They push away from a loop direction; they do not target `</think>`, and their evidence says the conditional part adds nothing for loop removal. Ours tests whether a different condition and a different write direction buy shorter answers.
5. **Steer2Edit and ThinkEdit** (4.3, 4.4). Training-free rank-1 edits for reasoning length. Their read side is the component's own alignment with the direction, so they rescale existing behaviour everywhere; there is no learned condition and no explicit action on `</think>`. Smallest model 1.5B (ThinkEdit, lengthening) and 4B (Steer2Edit, shortening).
6. **Probe-triggered early exit** (2.1, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 3.4, 3.5). Same signal family, executed by an inference hook that injects `</think>` and usually an answer prefix, often with patience rules or calibrated thresholds that a rank-1 edit cannot express.
7. **Logit-space stops** (3.2 Think Token Adjustment, 3.3 ThinkBrake). The unconditional and threshold versions of "raise `</think>`"; one logits processor each.

## Baselines we must compare against

Report for every row: accuracy, median and mean tokens, cap rate, share of answers that close the thinking block, accuracy among closed answers, and whether the answer keeps reasoning after the close. Same sampling, cap, grader, state dtype and presence-penalty window in all rows; pair differences by problem. Cheapest first.

1. **Thinking off** (model-card default). Known: 44.2 MATH-500 / 56.0 GSM8K; about 600 tokens on GSM8K.
2. **Decoding knobs, thinking on**: presence penalty 0 / 1.5 / 2.0; repetition penalty 1.1 (known: 23.1 / 42.8); temperature 1.0 (the model card's setting for thinking mode; I found no published number); n-gram blocking with a long n (32 rather than 8, per 4.2).
3. **Fixed thinking budget with forced `</think>`** (budget forcing), swept over the budget, for example 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k. Known for one unspecified budget: 35.4 / 56.4.
4. **Loop detector then commit**: when an n-gram loop is detected, force `</think>` or return the last parseable answer (5.5).
5. **`</think>` logit boost** at every step (Think Token Adjustment, sweep alpha) and **ThinkBrake** (stop when the margin to `</think>` is below a threshold at sentence boundaries).
6. **Marker-token logit penalty** on "Wait", "But", "Alternatively" and similar (5.5, 4.5).
7. **Text oracle and text rule**: inject `</think>` right after the eventual answer first appears (oracle, upper bound); and the "looks solid" style rule already counted in PLAN.md (49/200).
8. **Probe-triggered forced `</think>` at inference with our own `v`**: same read vector, same threshold, executed by a hook, with and without a patience rule (k consecutive firings). This isolates what baking the trigger into weights changes.
9. **Constant steering with our own `u`** at the same layers (no gate), at a few strengths, and the weight-space-lp steering vector (known: 50.8 on MATH-500). This tests whether the condition matters.
10. **Confidence and consistency exits**: DEER (trial answer at "Wait", threshold 0.95) and Dynasor (answer every 64 tokens, stop after N identical). Count the probe tokens.
11. **Existing weight edits on the same model**: the published edit (known: 48.1 / 67.1, median 11,202 tokens on our 200 GSM8K problems), the LP edit (51.7 / 69.6, mean 9,658 tokens on GSM8K), Weight Whisperer runs 1 and 2 (50.40 / 51.55 on MATH-500), and each of them with our edit added on top (PLAN.md already has the published-edit stack).
12. **Controls for our edit**: random `u` of the same norm; random `v` of the same norm and sparsity; `v` fitted on shuffled labels; `v` fitted to token position only; the constant-offset version of our edit (`u` times the mean of `v·h`).
